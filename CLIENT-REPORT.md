## 3. Three problems found and fixed along the way

These were not in your notes. They were found while testing and are worth knowing about.

### The logo was not appearing on the live site at all

The logo file was linked from the wrong address, so the website could not find it. Visitors were seeing plain text reading "ClearUp" instead of your actual logo. This is almost certainly why it looked hidden and undersized. It is fixed, and it is now larger in both the header and the footer.

### Closing a service pop-up froze the whole page

Clicking "Learn more" on any of the three service cards and then closing it left an invisible layer covering the page. Every click after that did nothing. The visitor could not request a quote, could not use the menu, could not click anything, and the only way out was to reload the page.

This was confirmed on the current live site, not just in testing. Anyone who opened a service description and closed it could not then request a quote. It is fixed.

### The quote form could lose everything in one click

The quote form runs to as many as 47 questions depending on the service. Clicking once anywhere outside the form erased all of it instantly, with no warning and nothing saved.

Now the answers save as they are entered, they come back if the form is reopened, and closing a part-filled form asks first rather than discarding it.

## 5. Things that need a decision from you

These were deliberately left alone because they are your words or your business facts, not design choices.

### The Management Systems section may be working against you

Two of the four panels currently read:

> "We are developing strong health and safety processes..."
> "We are building a quality management system..."

A facility manager reading that will understand it as: this contractor does not yet have a safety system. That is the section they read when deciding whether to put Clear Up on an approved contractor list, and it sits a short scroll below "Over 20 years of cleaning experience," which pulls in the opposite direction.

Also, the opening line now reads "These represent our professional standing in the cleaning industry." Because the certification sentence before it was removed, "These" no longer refers to anything.

**Suggested:** rewrite the four panels in the present tense to describe what already happens on site today. If the formal certifications are not held, the section could be retitled to something like "How We Run Every Site" and describe the actual practice instead. Happy to draft this once you confirm what is genuinely in place.

### There is no proof on the page

A prospective client is typically holding two or three quotes. The website currently offers no client names, no testimonial, no insurance cover amount, no ABN, no statement that cleaners are police-checked or inducted, and no photograph of a person, a site, a uniform or a vehicle.

This matters most because the page is asking a building manager to hand over keys and alarm codes. The quote form itself asks about key access and alarm codes, so the request is explicit, but nothing on the page reassures them about who is being let in.

**Suggested, in order of value:**
1. Two or three client names or site types, with permission. If names cannot be used, something specific still works: "12 retail sites across South East Queensland, cleaned nightly since 2019."
2. One short testimonial with a real name and role.
3. Public liability cover amount, ABN, and a line confirming staff are police-checked and inducted.
4. Photographs of real Clear Up sites, staff and vehicles.

For a business whose product is visible cleanliness, the absence of a single photograph is the largest single gap on the site.

### One change made on your behalf

The phone illustration in the app section showed a real shopping centre named as an active job. Because that implies a client relationship that has not been confirmed, it has been changed to a neutral site label. If that centre is genuinely a client and you are happy to name them, it can go back, and it would be strong proof.
